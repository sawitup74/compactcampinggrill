The Portable Fire Pit for Camping: Grill at Supper, Campfire After

How to use a raised steel charcoal grill as a small personal fire pit where allowed, and why it beats a shared ring.

A campfire is the center of camp. People gather around it, tell stories, warm their hands and roast marshmallows. But in many campgrounds, the only place to have one is a shared ring with a questionable history. A portable fire pit gives you a fire of your own. And when your portable fire pit is also your grill, you get two jobs out of one piece of gear.

What makes a good portable fire pit

  • Raised design so coals and wood are not sitting directly on the ground.
  • Thick steel that will not warp under repeated heating.
  • Airflow so the fire burns cleanly with less smoke.
  • Packability so it travels in a trunk, RV bay or boat.
  • Stability so it does not tip on uneven ground.

How the Slot Grill works as a fire pit

The Slot Grill is a charcoal grill that burns charcoal or cooking wood. After you've cooked dinner, add a few small splits of dry, local firewood to the remaining coals. It becomes a compact fire for warming hands and toasting marshmallows, sized for a small group gathered close.

The raised design keeps an air gap under the coal bed (2⅞" on the Compact, 3⅝" on the Full Size), which helps minimize radiant heat to the surface beneath. Both sizes are thick carbon steel: 12-gauge for the Compact and 10-gauge for the Full Size.

Rules first

Portable fire pits are treated differently in different places. Some parks allow them like any campfire, some only in designated areas, and some ban all fires during dry or windy conditions. Many burn bans include charcoal. Always check current rules with the land manager.

Setting up your fire pit safely

  • Level, non-combustible surface: gravel, bare dirt, a paver or a fire-safe mat.
  • At least 15 feet from tents, vehicles and trees.
  • No overhanging branches.
  • Water and shovel right next to it.

Fueling the fire

  • Use small, dry splits sized to fit the box.
  • Buy local firewood where required.
  • Do not overload; a small, hot fire is cleaner and safer.
  • Never burn trash, plastic, treated wood, or anything that is not clean fuel.

The sanitary advantage

Here is the thing nobody says about shared fire rings. They are used as trash cans, ashtrays and, after certain late nights, as urinals. Grates get caked in grease and ash and rarely see a brush. When the fire ring doubles as the campground grill, it all ends up under your dinner.

With your own fire pit and grill in one, you know exactly what has been in there: charcoal, clean wood, and your own cooking. Nothing else.

Putting it out

Let the fire burn down to ash. Drown with water, stir and drown again. Check with the back of your hand. Let the steel cool fully before packing.

Questions campers ask

Can a charcoal grill be used as a fire pit?

The Slot Grill burns charcoal or cooking wood, so where fires are permitted it can serve as a small fire pit after cooking.

Are portable fire pits allowed in campgrounds?

Rules vary. Check with the park, and note that burn bans may include charcoal.

What should I burn in a portable fire pit?

Only charcoal or clean, dry firewood, bought locally where required.
