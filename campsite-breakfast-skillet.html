Campsite Breakfast Skillet With Potatoes, Sausage and Eggs

A one-pan camp breakfast cooked in cast iron over charcoal, with make-ahead steps and variations.

There is a specific kind of hunger that only shows up at camp. You wake up cold, the coffee is still heating, and everyone is circling the cooler. This skillet answers that hunger with potatoes crisped in sausage drippings, peppers and onions, and eggs cooked right on top. It is one pan, it scales easily, and it uses up leftovers from the night before.

Why cast iron over charcoal

A cast iron skillet holds heat and spreads it evenly, which smooths out the hot spots of a small charcoal fire. It also needs a grill strong enough to hold it. Thin, flimsy grates sag under a loaded skillet; heavy steel does not. The Slot Grill Compact is made of 12-gauge carbon steel and is strong enough for cast iron, and the Full Size gives room for a big skillet plus extra space for toast.

Ingredients (serves 3 to 4)

  • 1 lb breakfast sausage, bulk or links sliced into coins
  • 1 1/2 lb small potatoes, par-cooked at home and quartered
  • 1 bell pepper, diced
  • 1 small onion, diced
  • 6 eggs
  • Salt, pepper, smoked paprika
  • Shredded cheese, hot sauce, tortillas (optional)

Make-ahead at home

Par-cooking the potatoes is the secret. Boil or microwave them until just tender, cool, quarter and bag them. They crisp at camp in a fraction of the time raw potatoes need. Dice the pepper and onion and bag them together. Crack the eggs into a sealed bottle if you prefer not to carry a carton, and keep them cold.

Fire setup

Light a medium batch of charcoal and spread it evenly under the grate area where the skillet will sit. You want medium heat: about a four-to-five-second hand count. If you plan to make toast or tortillas, keep a small cooler zone on one end.

Cooking steps

  1. Preheat the skillet on the grill for five minutes.
  2. Add the sausage and cook until browned, breaking it up if using bulk sausage.
  3. Push sausage to the side and add potatoes cut side down in the drippings. Leave them alone for a few minutes to crisp, then stir.
  4. Add peppers and onions with a pinch of salt. Cook until softened.
  5. Season with pepper and smoked paprika.
  6. Make wells in the hash and crack eggs into them. Cover with foil or a lid until the whites set.
  7. Top with cheese if you like and let it melt.

Timing at camp

From skillet on to eggs set takes roughly 25 to 30 minutes. Start the coffee water first, then the skillet. By the time breakfast is ready, the coffee is too.

Variations

  • Breakfast tacos: spoon the hash into warm tortillas heated on the grate.
  • Leftover steak: dice last night's steak and add it with the peppers.
  • Vegetarian: skip sausage, use butter and add mushrooms and spinach.
  • Southwest: add chopped green chiles and finish with salsa.

Cleanup without the mess

While the skillet is still warm, scrape out bits, add a splash of water and let it steam, then wipe it dry with a paper towel. Rub a thin coat of oil inside. Pack out the paper towel. Do not scrape grease into the fire ring or the dirt; that is how campsites end up smelling like a diner to every raccoon in the county.

Food safety

Cook sausage to the temperature recommended on the package; an instant-read thermometer takes the guessing out. Keep eggs cold until cooking and use them early in the trip.

The clean grill difference

Breakfast is the meal where people notice what the grate looks like, because the morning light shows everything. Setting your skillet on your own seasoned steel rather than a grate caked with someone else's late-night debris is a small luxury that sets the tone for the day.

Questions campers ask

Can I cook raw potatoes at camp?

Yes, but they take much longer. Par-cooking at home cuts cooking time dramatically.

Will a compact grill hold a cast iron skillet?

The Slot Grill Compact is described as strong enough for cast iron. Use a skillet sized to its 16.25 by 9 inch cooking area.

How do I keep eggs fresh while camping?

Keep them cold in the cooler, away from melting ice water, and use them in the first few days.
