Camping in the Ozarks and Arkansas

A regional highlight for Texas campers: the Buffalo National River, Hot Springs National Park and Ozark forests.

For Texas campers looking for a change of scenery within driving distance, Arkansas offers forested mountains, clear rivers and caves.

Buffalo National River

The first national river in the United States, flowing freely through the Ozarks with bluffs and gravel bars. Canoeing, kayaking and gravel bar camping are popular.

Hot Springs National Park

Known for its historic bathhouses and thermal springs, with a campground and trails.

Ozark National Forest

Mountains, waterfalls and trails across northern Arkansas.

Ouachita Mountains

Ridges and lakes in western Arkansas, home to the Ouachita National Forest.

Seasons

Fall color in the Ozarks is a highlight, and spring brings flowing waterfalls. Summer is ideal for river float trips.

Cooking on gravel bars

Gravel bar camping is a tradition on Ozark rivers. Where fires are allowed, a raised grill keeps coals off the gravel. Pack out cold ash rather than leaving black rings.

Questions campers ask

What is the Buffalo National River known for?

It was the first national river in the United States and is known for bluffs, float trips and gravel bar camping.

Is there camping at Hot Springs National Park?

The park has a campground; check current details with the park.

When is the best time to visit the Ozarks?

Fall for color and spring for waterfalls; summer for floating.
