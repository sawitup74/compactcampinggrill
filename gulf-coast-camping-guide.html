Gulf Coast Camping: Beaches, Bays and Barrier Islands

Where to camp along the Gulf of Mexico from Texas to Florida, plus beach camping tips for sand, wind and salt.

The Gulf Coast offers camping you can't find inland: falling asleep to the surf, waking to pelicans, and fishing right from camp. It also has its own challenges, including wind, sand, salt, bugs and hurricane season. This guide covers well-known destinations and how to camp happily by the water.

Texas coast

Padre Island National Seashore

The longest stretch of undeveloped barrier island in the world, south of Corpus Christi. It is known for primitive beach camping, birding, and its sea turtle program, including nesting Kemp's ridley sea turtles. Four-wheel drive is needed for much of the beach beyond the developed area.

Mustang Island State Park

Beach camping near Port Aransas and Corpus Christi, with bay-side paddling.

Galveston Island State Park

Beach and bay on one island near Houston, popular for fishing and birding.

Sea Rim State Park

Near the Louisiana border, where marsh meets the Gulf, known for birding and paddling trails.

Louisiana

Grand Isle State Park

On Louisiana's only inhabited barrier island, popular for fishing and birding.

Alabama and Mississippi

Gulf State Park

On the Alabama coast near Gulf Shores, with beaches, lakes and trails.

Gulf Islands National Seashore

Stretching across parts of Mississippi and Florida, with barrier islands, historic forts and white-sand beaches.

Florida Panhandle

Florida's Panhandle is known for sugar-white sand and emerald water, with a number of state parks offering beach and forest camping.

Beach camping tips

Sand

  • Use sand stakes, longer and wider than standard stakes, or bury "deadman" anchors.
  • Keep a brush and a bucket of water near the tent door for feet.
  • Keep zippers closed.

Wind

  • Orient the tent's narrow end into the wind.
  • Pitch low-profile shelters.
  • Weigh down everything; it will blow away.

Salt and sun

  • Rinse gear with fresh water after the trip.
  • Shade is scarce; bring a canopy rated for wind.
  • Wear sunscreen and reapply often.

Bugs

Mosquitoes and biting flies can be fierce on calm evenings near marshes. Bring repellent, head nets and a tent with good mesh.

Cooking on the beach

Beach fires are allowed in some places and banned in others. Check local rules. Where charcoal is permitted, a raised steel grill keeps coals contained and off the sand. Never bury coals in sand; they stay hot for hours and burn bare feet. Drown them with water and pack the cold ash out.

The compact size of a flat-pack grill pays off at the beach. Fresh seafood at camp

Grill fresh Gulf shrimp on skewers, blackened redfish, or whole snapper. Keep seafood on ice, cook it early, and dispose of scraps as local rules direct. Never leave fish scraps near camp.

Hurricane season

The Atlantic hurricane season runs June through November. Watch forecasts, follow park closures, and leave early if a storm threatens.

Questions campers ask

Can I camp on the beach in Texas?

Yes, in places such as Padre Island National Seashore and some state parks. Rules vary by location.

How do I stake a tent in sand?

Use long sand stakes or bury anchors such as stuff sacks filled with sand.

Are beach fires allowed on the Gulf Coast?

It depends on the location. Check local rules, and never bury hot coals in sand.
