Camp Steak and Foil-Free Potatoes on a Small Grill

Two-zone grilled steak and skewered potatoes cooked over charcoal at camp, without foil packets to pack out.

Steak at camp feels like a celebration. It is also easy to ruin, especially over a crowded public grate set at whatever height the park installed it. This recipe uses a two-zone charcoal fire on a compact grill so you can sear hard and then finish gently, and it cooks potatoes on skewers instead of in foil packets that always seem to end up half-burned and littering the site.

Picking the cut

Ribeye and strip are forgiving and flavorful. Sirloin is a budget-friendly choice. Aim for steaks about an inch to an inch and a half thick. Thin steaks overcook before they brown; very thick steaks take longer than hungry campers will wait.

Ingredients (serves 2 to 3)

  • 2 steaks, about 1 to 1.5 inches thick
  • Coarse salt and black pepper
  • 1 lb baby potatoes, par-cooked at home
  • Olive oil, garlic powder, dried rosemary
  • Butter (optional)
  • Metal skewers

Prep

Salt the steaks generously an hour before cooking if you can and leave them in the cooler, then pull them out while the coals heat. Toss the par-cooked potatoes with oil, garlic powder and rosemary, and thread them onto skewers.

Build a two-zone fire

Light charcoal and pile most of it on one end of the grill. Leave the other end with a thin layer or none. On the Slot Grill Compact, with its 16.25-inch long cooking area, that leaves room for steaks on the hot side and potato skewers on the cooler side.

Cook the potatoes first

Put the skewers on the cooler side. Turn every few minutes until browned and crispy, 12 to 15 minutes. Because they were par-cooked, you are only crisping and heating, not cooking through.

Sear the steaks

  1. Pat steaks dry and add pepper.
  2. Lay them on the hot side and leave them alone for two to three minutes.
  3. Flip and sear the second side.
  4. Move to the cooler side to finish.
  5. Check with an instant-read thermometer. Pull about 5 degrees below your target, since temperature rises while resting.

Rest and serve

Rest steaks five minutes on a board. Top with a pat of butter. Slice against the grain and serve with the potatoes.

Why skip the foil packets

Foil packets are a camping tradition, but they have problems. They tear, they leak, they burn on the bottom while the top stays raw, and the used foil becomes trash that often gets thrown in the fire ring, where it does not burn. Skewers or a skillet give better browning and leave nothing behind.

Grill height and why it matters

Many park grills are fixed in place, either too high to sear or so low the meat sits in flames. A compact grill with the grate at a set distance over a measured charcoal bed gives you control. You decide how much heat by how many coals you use and where you put them.

Variations

  • Steak tips: cube sirloin and skewer with onions and peppers.
  • Chimichurri: make the sauce at home; it keeps well in the cooler.
  • Cast iron sear: heat a skillet on the hot side and sear in butter for a crust.

Questions campers ask

What is the best steak for camping?

Ribeye and strip steaks are forgiving and flavorful; sirloin is a solid budget pick.

How long should I rest a steak?

About five minutes is enough for most camp steaks.

Do I need a thermometer?

It removes guesswork, especially at night. An instant-read thermometer is light and fast.
