Gulf Coast Camping: Beaches, Bays and Barrier Islands

Where to camp along the Gulf of Mexico from Texas to Florida, plus beach camping tips for sand, wind and salt.

The Gulf Coast offers camping you can't find inland: falling asleep to the surf, waking to pelicans, and fishing right from camp. It also has its own challenges, including wind, sand, salt, bugs and hurricane season. This guide covers well-known destinations and how to camp happily by the water.

Texas coast

Padre Island National Seashore

The longest stretch of undeveloped barrier island in the world, south of Corpus Christi. It is known for primitive beach camping, birding, and its sea turtle program, including nesting Kemp's ridley sea turtles. Four-wheel drive is needed for much of the beach beyond the developed area.

Mustang Island State Park

Beach camping near Port Aransas and Corpus Christi, with bay-side paddling.

Galveston Island State Park

Beach and bay on one island near Houston, popular for fishing and birding.

Sea Rim State Park

Near the Louisiana border, where marsh meets the Gulf, known for birding and paddling trails.

Louisiana

Grand Isle State Park

On Louisiana's only inhabited barrier island, popular for fishing and birding.

Alabama and Mississippi

Gulf State Park

On the Alabama coast near Gulf Shores, with beaches, lakes and trails.

Gulf Islands National Seashore

Stretching across parts of Mississippi and Florida, with barrier islands, historic forts and white-sand beaches.

Florida Panhandle

Florida's Panhandle is known for sugar-white sand and emerald water, with a number of state parks offering beach and forest camping.

Beach camping tips

Sand

  • Use sand stakes, longer and wider than standard stakes, or bury "deadman" anchors.
  • Keep a brush and a bucket of water near the tent door for feet.
  • Keep zippers closed.

Wind

  • Orient the tent's narrow end into the wind.
  • Pitch low-profile shelters.
  • Weigh down everything; it will blow away.

Salt and sun

  • Rinse gear with fresh water after the trip.
  • Shade is scarce; bring a canopy rated for wind.
  • Wear sunscreen and reapply often.

Bugs

Mosquitoes and biting flies can be fierce on calm evenings near marshes. Bring repellent, head nets and a tent with good mesh.

Cooking on the beach

Beach fires are allowed in some places and banned in others. Check local rules. Where charcoal is permitted, a raised steel grill keeps coals contained and off the sand. Never bury coals in sand; they stay hot for hours and burn bare feet. Drown them with water and pack the cold ash out.

Fresh seafood at camp

Grill fresh Gulf shrimp on skewers, blackened redfish, or whole snapper. Keep seafood on ice, cook it early, and dispose of scraps as local rules direct. Never leave fish scraps near camp.

Hurricane season

The Atlantic hurricane season runs June through November. Watch forecasts, follow park closures, and leave early if a storm threatens.

Choosing a beach campsite

  • Above the high tide line with plenty of margin.
  • Away from dunes and vegetation, which are fragile.
  • Wind protection if possible.
  • Close to parking to carry gear.

Beach camping gear

  • Sand stakes
  • A wind-rated canopy
  • Sand-free mat for the tent door
  • Bucket for rinsing feet
  • Beach cart for gear
  • Cooler with extra ice
  • Shade structure

Fishing the Gulf

Surf fishing, bay fishing and pier fishing are popular. Follow licensing rules and size and bag limits.

Wildlife on the coast

  • Sea turtles: follow nesting season rules, avoid lights on the beach at night where required, and report nesting turtles where requested.
  • Birds: the Gulf Coast is a major bird migration route.
  • Jellyfish: watch for them in the water.

Seafood cooking at the beach

  • Shrimp: grill on skewers.
  • Redfish: grill on the half shell (scales on) where legal.
  • Oysters: grill until they open.

Beach fire rules

Rules vary widely by beach, county and season, and some beaches ban fires completely. Check before you go.

Leave No Trace on the beach

Pack out all trash, fill in holes, and remove all fire debris.

Best seasons on the coast

  • Fall: often the most pleasant season, with cooler days and fewer crowds, though hurricane season continues into November.
  • Winter: mild days, cool and windy nights, and few bugs. Northers can bring sudden cold.
  • Spring: warming water, spring migration birding and busy spring break weeks.
  • Summer: hot and humid, with afternoon storms and peak hurricane season risk later in summer.

Hurricane awareness

Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30. Watch the forecast before and during your trip, know your evacuation route, and leave early when officials advise. Coastal campgrounds can close quickly ahead of storms.

Camping on sand

  • Use long sand stakes or bury deadman anchors (stuff sacks filled with sand).
  • Pitch low-profile tents and orient the narrow end to the wind.
  • Keep sand out of the tent with a brush and a mat at the door.
  • Set the grill on a firm, level spot; a flat board or paver can help on soft sand.

Beach fire rules

Fire rules vary widely along the Gulf. Some beaches allow contained fires, others prohibit them entirely, and rules change with conditions. Ask locally and follow posted rules. Where fires are allowed, a raised grill keeps coals off the sand, and you can let it cool completely and pack out the ash rather than burying hot coals, which can burn bare feet long after.

Bugs and how to deal with them

Mosquitoes and biting flies can be intense, especially near marshes and at dusk. Bring repellent, a screen shelter, and long, light-colored clothing. Breezy sites have fewer bugs.

Coastal cooking

The coast is the place for seafood. Buy fresh shrimp, fish or oysters from local markets when you can. Shrimp skewers, blackened fish and grilled oysters are classic Gulf meals.

Rinsing and protecting gear

Salt air and sand are hard on gear. Rinse the grill with fresh water after the trip, dry it thoroughly, heat it and re-season. Rinse zippers, stakes and chairs too.

Wildlife on the coast

  • Give nesting birds and sea turtles plenty of space; follow posted closures.
  • Don't feed gulls or other wildlife.
  • Watch for jellyfish and stingrays when wading; shuffle your feet.
  • Keep food secured from raccoons.

Sun and heat

Reflected light from sand and water increases sun exposure. Use shade, sunscreen, hats and sunglasses, and drink plenty of water.

Fishing and crabbing

The Gulf Coast offers surf fishing, bay fishing and crabbing. Check licensing and regulations for the state where you're fishing, and follow size and bag limits.

Tides

Know high and low tide times before setting up near the water. Set camp well above the high tide line.

Coastal camp kitchen

Set the grill on firm ground out of the wind. Keep the cooler shaded and lidded. Rinse everything with fresh water at the end of the trip.

Choosing a site on the coast

Look for sites with some wind protection, shade, and firm ground. Sites near dunes can be windy and sandy, while bayside sites may be buggier.

Leaving the beach clean

Pack out every bit of trash, including food scraps and fishing line, which can entangle birds and turtles. Fill in holes dug in the sand, which can trap nesting turtles and trip other visitors.

Gulf seafood on the grill

Buy fresh shrimp, fish or oysters from a local market when you can. Shrimp grill in just minutes on skewers or on the griddle. Oysters can be set on the grate, cup side down, until they open slightly, then topped with butter and hot sauce. Keep seafood on ice until cooking, and cook it the same day you buy it.

One more tip

Bring a bucket of fresh water for rinsing sandy feet before getting in the tent.

Questions campers ask

Can I camp on the beach in Texas?

Yes, in places such as Padre Island National Seashore and some state parks. Rules vary by location.

How do I stake a tent in sand?

Use long sand stakes or bury anchors such as stuff sacks filled with sand.

Are beach fires allowed on the Gulf Coast?

It depends on the location. Check local rules, and never bury hot coals in sand.
