RV Cooking Outdoors: Keep the Heat and Smell Out of the Rig

Why cooking outside the camper is better, and how to set up an outdoor kitchen at an RV site.

Cooking inside an RV heats it up, fills it with smells and coats surfaces with grease. Cooking outside keeps your rig clean and cool, and it's more fun.

  • Your grill on a stable, non-combustible surface away from the rig and awning
  • A cooler or use of the fridge for cold storage
  • A wash tub

Why outdoor cooking is better for RVers

RV kitchens are small, and every meal cooked inside leaves behind heat, humidity and smell. In summer, a stovetop meal can push the interior temperature up enough to keep the air conditioner running for hours. Frying fills the rig with grease that settles on cabinets and fabric. Cooking outside avoids all of it, and you get to enjoy the campground instead of staring at a microwave.

Building an outdoor RV kitchen kit

Keep a dedicated bin in an exterior storage bay so outdoor cooking never means digging through the rig:

  • Flat-pack grill and a lidded bucket of charcoal
  • Chimney starter or starter cubes, lighter, matches
  • Folding table and a cutting board
  • Tongs, spatula, heat-resistant gloves, instant-read thermometer
  • Cast iron skillet or a griddle plate
  • Paper towels, trash bags and a small dish tub
  • A battery lantern or headlamp hook
  • Fire extinguisher (in addition to the one inside the rig)

Where to place the grill at a typical site

Most RV sites have a parking pad, a picnic table, and a fire ring or pedestal grill. Your grill should go:

  • On a level, non-combustible surface such as the concrete pad, gravel or a fire-safe mat
  • Well clear of the RV body, slide-outs and awning, with extra space on the windward side
  • Away from propane tanks, regulators and hoses
  • Downwind of where people sit and of open windows and vents

If the site has a picnic table, check campground rules and the table material before setting a grill on it. The Slot Grill's raised design leaves an air gap under the coal bed that helps minimize radiant heat to the surface below, but a non-combustible surface is still the right call.

The awning rule

Never cook under an awning, even on a rainy day. Awning fabric can melt or ignite from rising heat and sparks, and smoke trapped under it drifts straight into the rig. If you want rain cover, rig a separate tarp high above and off to the side of the grill, with plenty of open air around it.

Carbon monoxide awareness

Charcoal produces carbon monoxide. It must never be used inside the RV, inside a screen room or tent, or in an enclosed garage area of a toy hauler. Make sure your rig's CO detector works and has fresh batteries. Keep the grill far enough from open windows and vents that smoke and fumes do not drift inside.

Cleaning without wasting tank space

Grey tanks fill fast. Scrape plates into the trash before washing, use a dish tub outside, and wipe greasy pans with paper towels before they ever see water. Dispose of grey water as the campground directs. A carbon steel grill should be scraped warm and wiped, not washed with lots of water.

Storage on the road

A flat-pack grill earns its place in an RV because it is thin. The Compact packs to about an inch thick and the Full Size to about an inch and a quarter, so either slides behind a bin or along the wall of a storage bay. Let the grill cool completely and wipe it dry before packing it, and store charcoal in a sealed container so road vibration does not create a bay full of black dust.

Etiquette at busy RV parks

  • Keep smoke to a minimum: light charcoal fully before cooking and avoid wet wood.
  • Cook at reasonable hours.
  • Never leave a grill burning unattended while you head to the pool.
  • Drown and cool coals before bed.

Saving propane and power

Cooking over charcoal outside saves the RV's propane for heating, the fridge and hot water. It also avoids running the vent fan and air conditioner hard to clear heat and smoke from a small interior. On hookup sites, it keeps the coach cooler on hot afternoons.

Campground rules for RV cooking

Many campgrounds allow charcoal grills on the site pad but may restrict them during burn bans or prohibit them in certain areas. Ask at check-in and follow posted rules. Some RV parks ask that cooking stay on the concrete pad, never on grass.

Keeping the RV clean

  • Prep raw meat outside on a folding table.
  • Use a wash bin outdoors rather than the coach sink for greasy pans.
  • Wipe the grill outside and store it in a bag or bin before it goes in a storage bay.
  • Keep a separate towel for outdoor cooking.

Storing the grill on the road

A flat-pack grill stacks under a mattress or slides into a basement bay without rattling. Wrap it in an old towel or store it in a bag to keep soot off other gear, and make sure it's cool and dry before packing.

Cooking at rest stops and boondocking

When you boondock on public land where fires and charcoal are allowed, a raised grill keeps coals contained and off the ground. Check local fire rules first, especially in dry western areas.

Outdoor cooking gear list for RVers

Grill, griddle, charcoal, chimney starter, gloves, thermometer, tongs, spatula, foil, a folding table and a wash bin.

Cooking for neighbors at the RV park

RV parks are social places, and the smell of something good on the grill tends to bring people over. A Full Size grill makes it easy to cook extra and share with neighbors, which is one of the best ways to meet fellow travelers. Bring extra plates and forks, and plan a potluck night if you're staying for a while.

Rainy-day RV cooking

When rain sets in, keep the grill out in the open, never under the awning, and cook quick meals like smash burgers or quesadillas on the griddle. Wear a rain jacket and keep a towel by the door. If storms bring lightning, wait them out inside and switch to a no-cook meal.

A final note

Questions campers ask

Should I cook inside or outside my RV?

Outside keeps the rig cooler and cleaner.

Where should I put a grill at an RV site?

On a stable, non-combustible surface away from the RV, awning and propane connections.

Can I use charcoal in a screen room?

No. Charcoal must be used in open air.
