Campfire, Safety & Leave No Trace

Leave No Trace for Everyday Campers

The seven Leave No Trace principles translated into practical habits at the campsite, trailhead and cook area.

Leave No Trace is a set of seven principles that help people enjoy the outdoors without wearing it out. It was not written only for backcountry purists. The same ideas apply at a crowded drive-in campground, a lakeside paddle camp or a trailer park with full hookups. Here is what each principle looks like in real camping life.

1. Plan ahead and prepare

Most damage outdoors comes from people who did not expect what they found. They arrive after dark, can't find the site, and pitch a tent on the vegetation. They forget a trash bag and leave wrappers behind. They bring no water container and cannot put out their fire properly.

  • Read park alerts for closures and fire restrictions.
  • Pack trash bags, a water jug for fire, and a small shovel.
  • Repackage food at home to cut down on packaging waste.
  • Plan meals so you are not carrying leftovers that attract wildlife.

2. Travel and camp on durable surfaces

Durable surfaces include established campsites, trails, rock, gravel, dry grass and sand. In developed campgrounds, keep your tent on the tent pad and your vehicle on the parking spur. At dispersed sites, use places that are already obviously used rather than creating new clearings.

Keep camps at least 200 feet from lakes and streams in the backcountry where possible. In campgrounds, the site layout makes that decision for you.

3. Dispose of waste properly

"Pack it in, pack it out" covers food, wrappers, foil and broken gear. Dishwater should be strained of food bits, and the bits packed out. Scatter strained gray water away from water sources, or use the campground's designated drain.

Human waste is a topic nobody loves, but it matters. Use restrooms where they exist. In the backcountry, dig a cathole six to eight inches deep, at least 200 feet from water, trails and camp. Pack out toilet paper where regulations require it.

4. Leave what you find

Rocks, plants, antlers, shells and artifacts stay where they are. Don't build furniture out of logs or dig trenches around tents. The point is that the next camper gets to discover the same place you did.

5. Minimize campfire impacts

This is where camp cooking and Leave No Trace meet. Fires scar the ground, consume wood that animals and soil rely on, and leave ugly charcoal rings when built carelessly.

  • Use existing fire rings where fires are allowed, or a raised fire container.
  • Keep fires small.
  • Use only dead and down wood where collection is permitted, or bring local firewood.
  • Burn wood to ash, put it out completely, and scatter cool ash where appropriate.

A raised steel grill that holds your charcoal or wood is a low-impact option. It keeps coals off the soil and lets you pack out ash rather than leaving a new black scar. Just set it on a durable, non-combustible surface and follow local rules.

6. Respect wildlife

Watch animals from a distance. Never feed them, even the cute ones begging at the picnic table. Store food, coolers and trash in vehicles, lockers or bear-resistant containers as required. Grease and food scraps left in a fire ring teach animals that campsites mean dinner.

7. Be considerate of other visitors

Keep noise down, especially after quiet hours. Keep generators within campground rules. Let others enjoy the view without your speaker. Keep pets leashed and clean up after them.

Leave No Trace in the camp kitchen

Your kitchen is where most traces get left. A few habits make a big difference:

  • Cook on your own clean grill and pack everything out.
  • Use a tub for dishes and strain the water.
  • Wipe grease with a paper towel and pack it out instead of dumping it.
  • Keep a small trash bag clipped to the table so wrappers never blow away.
  • After cooking, let coals burn down, drown them with water, and confirm they are cold before packing ash.

Teaching kids Leave No Trace

Kids are excellent rule enforcers once they understand the why. Make it a game: who can find the most micro-trash around the site before you leave? Give them the job of checking that the fire is cold with the back of their hand (after an adult confirms it is out). Turn "leave what you find" into a photo scavenger hunt.

A Leave No Trace campsite checklist

Before you leave:

  1. All trash packed.
  2. Micro-trash picked up.
  3. Fire dead out and cold.
  4. Ash disposed properly.
  5. No food scraps left.
  6. Tent area restored.
  7. Picnic table wiped clean.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving foil and cans in the fire ring
  • Dumping grease on the ground
  • Washing dishes in lakes or streams
  • Feeding wildlife
  • Breaking branches for firewood

Why it matters

Campsites that get trashed get closed. Clean camping keeps places open for everyone.

The seven principles in camp terms

  1. Plan ahead and prepare. Know the rules, the weather and the fire situation before you go, and bring the gear to follow them.
  2. Travel and camp on durable surfaces. Use existing sites, pads and trails rather than creating new ones.
  3. Dispose of waste properly. Pack it in, pack it out, including food scraps and micro-trash.
  4. Leave what you find. Rocks, plants, artifacts and antlers stay where they are.
  5. Minimize campfire impacts. Use existing rings or a raised, contained fire pit, keep fires small, and burn only clean fuel.
  6. Respect wildlife. Observe from a distance and never feed animals.
  7. Be considerate of other visitors. Keep noise down and share spaces.

Micro-trash

Twist ties, bottle caps, foil corners, bread bag clips and food crumbs are the trash most often left behind. Do a slow sweep of your site before leaving, at eye level close to the ground.

Gray water

Strain food bits out of dishwater and pack them out with trash. Scatter the strained water well away from camp and at least 200 feet from lakes and streams where no gray water drain is provided.

Fire impacts

Fire scars soil for years. Rings filled with half-burned trash, cans and food are among the most common eyesores at campgrounds. Burning trash releases toxic smoke and leaves debris that never burns away. A raised steel grill used as a fire pit keeps fire off the ground entirely, and once the ash is cold you can carry it to a proper disposal spot.

Wildlife and food

Animals that get human food become bold and can become dangerous. Store all food, trash and scented items securely. Never leave food out at night.

Noise and light

Respect quiet hours, keep music low, and point lights downward.

Pets

Keep pets leashed, clean up after them, and don't let them chase wildlife.

Teaching kids

Kids learn by example. Show them how to pick up micro-trash, stay on trails, and leave nature as they found it.

Leaving it better

Pick up trash left by others when you can. Small acts like this make campgrounds better for everyone.

Cooking with Leave No Trace in mind

Camp cooking creates more waste and impact than any other part of a trip, so plan your kitchen around it. Repackage food at home to cut down on trash. Cook only what you'll eat to avoid leftovers you can't store. Strain dishwater and pack out the scraps. Keep grease in a sealed can, never poured on the ground. Use a raised grill on a durable surface so coals never scar the soil, and clean the grate after cooking so food bits don't fall into the dirt. When you leave, the only sign you cooked there should be the memory of a good meal.

One more tip

Teach others by example; most people follow good habits when they see them in action.

Questions campers ask

Does Leave No Trace apply in developed campgrounds?

Yes. The principles help keep busy campgrounds clean and safe for wildlife and the next visitors.

What should I do with cold campfire ash?

Use the campground's ash disposal if provided. Otherwise pack it out in a metal container. Make sure it is completely cool first.

Is a raised fire container better for Leave No Trace?

A raised steel container keeps coals off the soil and lets you pack out ash, which can reduce impact compared to building a new fire on the ground.
