Camping in the Big Bend Region of Texas

Big Bend National Park, Big Bend Ranch State Park and the Davis Mountains: desert camping, dark skies and planning for remote country.

Far West Texas is a different world: Chihuahuan Desert, rugged mountains, the Rio Grande, and some of the darkest skies in the country. Camping here is unforgettable, but it requires preparation. Services are far apart, water is scarce, and weather can be extreme.

Big Bend National Park

The national park sits in the big bend of the Rio Grande. Its landscapes range from desert floor to the Chisos Mountains, which rise in the park's center, to the river canyons such as Santa Elena Canyon. The park has developed campgrounds and backcountry camping by permit.

Big Bend Ranch State Park

West of the national park, Big Bend Ranch is one of the largest state parks in Texas. It is remote and rugged, with primitive camping, backcountry roads and trails.

Davis Mountains State Park

North of the Big Bend region near Fort Davis, the Davis Mountains offer higher elevations, cooler temperatures and grasslands. The McDonald Observatory nearby hosts star parties.

Dark skies

The region is known for exceptional stargazing. Plan around the new moon for the darkest nights.

Planning essentials

  • Fill fuel tanks at every opportunity.
  • Carry plenty of water, more than you think you need.
  • Check road conditions; some backcountry roads require high-clearance vehicles.
  • Cell service is limited.
  • Summer heat on the desert floor can be dangerous; spring, fall and winter are popular seasons.

Fire and cooking

Fire rules in the desert are strict because vegetation is fragile and fires spread fast. Many areas ban ground fires entirely, and backcountry rules may limit what is allowed. Check current regulations before you go.

Where your type of grill is permitted, a raised steel grill keeps coals contained and off the desert floor. Pack out all ash and charcoal; nothing should be left behind.

Wildlife

Expect javelinas, roadrunners, and many bird species. Store food securely; javelinas and other animals are known to raid unattended camps.

Leave No Trace

Desert soils and plants recover slowly. Stay on durable surfaces and pack out everything.

Questions campers ask

When is the best time to camp in Big Bend?

Spring, fall and winter are popular; summer on the desert floor can be extremely hot.

Can I have a campfire in Big Bend National Park?

Ground fires are generally prohibited. Check the park's current rules for grills and stoves.

What is Big Bend known for?

Desert and mountain landscapes, the Rio Grande canyons and exceptional dark skies.
