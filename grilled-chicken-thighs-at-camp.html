Grilled Chicken Thighs at Camp

Juicy bone-in or boneless thighs over two-zone charcoal, with a simple rub and a thermometer check.

Chicken thighs are the most forgiving chicken for camp grilling. They stay juicy, take on smoke beautifully and are hard to overcook.

Ingredients

  • 2 lb chicken thighs
  • Oil
  • Rub: 1 tsp each salt, paprika, garlic powder, brown sugar, and 1/2 tsp pepper

Prep

Mix the rub at home. At camp, pat thighs dry, oil, and season.

Cooking

  1. Set up a two-zone fire.
  2. Start skin side down on the cooler side.
  3. Cook covered with foil or a pan lid.
  4. Move to the hot side to crisp the skin.
  5. Cook to 165°F; many cooks prefer thighs a bit higher for tenderness.

Serving

With grilled corn, potato skewers or tortillas.

Food safety

Keep raw chicken sealed and at the bottom of the cooler. Wash hands and boards after handling.

Bone-in or boneless?

Bone-in, skin-on thighs are the most forgiving cut you can put on a camp grill. The bone slows cooking and the skin renders into a crisp, salty crust. They take longer, so plan on dinner at dusk rather than a quick bite.

Boneless, skinless thighs cook in roughly half the time and are easy to slice for tacos, wraps and rice bowls. They can dry out on very high heat, so keep them over medium coals.

Three camp-friendly flavor profiles

Texas dry rub

Mix 1 tablespoon each of brown sugar and paprika, 2 teaspoons each of salt and garlic powder, 1 teaspoon each of black pepper and chili powder, and a pinch of cayenne. Jar it at home.

Lemon garlic herb

Whisk olive oil, the juice and zest of two lemons, four cloves of minced garlic, dried oregano, salt and pepper. Marinate overnight in a sealed bag in the cooler.

Sweet and smoky glaze

Season with salt and pepper, grill until nearly done, then brush with barbecue sauce for the last few minutes on the cooler side so the sugar does not burn.

Detailed cooking timeline (bone-in)

  1. 0:00 Light charcoal in a chimney or with starter cubes.
  2. 0:25 Spread coals to one end for a two-zone fire.
  3. 0:30 Place thighs skin side up on the cooler side; cover loosely with a foil tent or an upside-down pan.
  4. 0:50 Flip skin side down, still on the cool side, to begin rendering fat.
  5. 1:00 Check temperature. When thighs read in the mid-150s°F, move them skin side down over the hot coals for two to four minutes to crisp. Watch for flare-ups.
  6. 1:05 Confirm at least 165°F in the thickest part, not touching bone.
  7. 1:10 Rest five minutes and serve.

Boneless thighs follow the same idea, compressed: about 5 to 7 minutes per side over medium heat.

Taming flare-ups

Chicken fat dripping onto coals causes flames. Keep a cool zone so you can move pieces away from fire instantly. Do not spray water on hot coals; it throws ash onto the food. Trimming large flaps of excess skin and fat at home reduces flare-ups.

What to serve alongside

  • Corn on the cob grilled in the husk on the cool side
  • Skewered par-cooked potatoes
  • Tortillas warmed on the grate with salsa and cabbage
  • Rice cooked in a pot on the edge of the grill
  • A cold cucumber and tomato salad from the cooler

Cooking for more people

The Slot Grill Compact is sized for one to three people, so a few thighs per person fit in a single round. For bigger groups, cook in two rounds and hold finished chicken on the cool side under foil, or step up to the Full Size for a family in one batch.

Storage and leftovers

Cool leftovers quickly by spreading them in a container in the cooler. Use within a day. Sliced cold chicken makes an excellent lunch on day two, tucked into wraps with cheese and hot sauce.

Raw poultry safety checklist

  • Pack raw chicken in a sealed container at the very bottom of the cooler.
  • Use a dedicated cutting board or the inside of its packaging for raw meat.
  • Keep separate tongs for raw and cooked chicken, or wash them between uses.
  • Wash hands with soap after handling; wet wipes alone are not enough.
  • Never put cooked chicken back on the plate that held it raw.

Troubleshooting

  • Burnt outside, raw inside: heat too high. Use the cool side for most of the cook.
  • Rubbery skin: finish over direct heat to crisp it.
  • Dry meat: you likely overcooked boneless thighs. Pull them right at a safe temperature and rest.

Why thighs beat breasts at camp

Thighs are forgiving. They stay juicy even if overcooked slightly, handle high heat well, and take marinades beautifully. They are also typically less expensive.

Bone-in vs. boneless

  • Boneless: quicker, easier, more even cooking.
  • Bone-in, skin-on: more flavor and crispy skin, but longer cooking and more care to avoid flare-ups.

Marinades

  • Lemon garlic: lemon juice, olive oil, garlic, oregano.
  • Teriyaki: soy sauce, ginger, garlic, honey.
  • Chipotle lime: chipotle in adobo, lime, garlic, cumin.
  • Yogurt herb: plain yogurt, garlic, lemon, dill.

Cooking method

  1. Set up two heat zones.
  2. Sear thighs over the hot zone.
  3. Move to the cooler side to cook through.
  4. Check temperature with a thermometer.
  5. Rest briefly before serving.

Doneness

Chicken is safe at 165°F, but thighs are often more tender cooked a little higher, to around 175°F, as connective tissue breaks down.

Leftovers

Leftover thighs make great tacos, wraps or salads the next day. Keep them cold.

Food safety

Keep raw chicken cold and separate. Use separate plates and utensils for raw and cooked chicken. Wash hands after handling raw meat.

Sides

  • Grilled vegetables
  • Rice
  • Corn on the cob
  • Salad

Troubleshooting

  • Flare-ups: move chicken to the cool side.
  • Burnt outside, raw inside: heat too high; cook longer on the cool side.
  • Dry meat: overcooked; check with a thermometer.

Prepping at home

Trim excess fat and skin at home, where you have a sink and good knives. Put the thighs in a heavy zip bag with marinade, squeeze out the air, and freeze flat. They thaw in the cooler by the second day and marinate as they thaw. Pack the bag inside a second bag to prevent leaks onto other food.

Two-zone cooking in detail

Bank most of the lit coals on one side of the grill. Sear thighs skin side down over the hot side for a few minutes until well browned. When fat drips and flares, slide them to the cool side, skin side up, and let them finish gently. Bone-in thighs usually need 25 to 35 minutes total, while boneless thighs often finish in 10 to 15. Your thermometer is the final judge.

Glazes and finishing touches

Brush on sugary sauces like barbecue sauce or honey glazes only in the last few minutes, so they caramelize without burning. A squeeze of lime, a scatter of chopped herbs, or a dusting of flaky salt right before serving makes simple chicken taste special.

Chicken thigh tacos

Slice grilled boneless thighs into strips and serve in warm tortillas with diced onion, cilantro, salsa and lime. Grill the tortillas briefly on the grate for char. This turns a basic grilled chicken dinner into a taco night with almost no extra work.

Keeping it cold until cooking

Raw chicken should stay at or below 40°F until it hits the grill. Keep it at the bottom of the cooler, close to the ice, and don't leave it sitting out while the coals heat.

A simple spice rub

If you don't want to fuss with a marinade, a dry rub works well. Mix brown sugar, paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, salt, pepper and a pinch of cayenne in a small jar at home. Pat the thighs dry, rub generously, and let them sit in the cooler for an hour or more before grilling. Watch the heat, since sugar in the rub can scorch over the hottest coals.

One more tip

Always use a clean plate for cooked chicken, never the one that held it raw.

Questions campers ask

How long do chicken thighs take on a camp grill?

About 20 to 30 minutes depending on size and heat.

What temperature should chicken thighs reach?

At least 165°F.

How do I keep raw chicken safe at camp?

Seal it and keep it at the bottom of a cold cooler.

Should I grill bone-in or boneless thighs at camp?

Bone-in thighs are more forgiving and flavorful but slower; boneless cook faster and are easy to slice.

How do I stop flare-ups with chicken?

Keep a cool zone and move pieces off direct heat when fat drips; trim excess fat at home.
