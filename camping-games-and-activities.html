Camping Games and Activities for Kids and Families

Campsite games, nature activities and fireside fun for all ages, with no screens required.

Half the magic of family camping is the unstructured time: hours with nothing scheduled but exploring. Still, a few games in your pocket can turn a restless afternoon or a long evening into a highlight. Here are tried-and-true camping activities.

Daytime games

Nature scavenger hunt

Make a list: something smooth, something with a smell, a feather, a pinecone, a red leaf, something a bird made. Kids find and photograph items, leaving them in place.

Bird bingo

Make bingo cards with local birds. Bring binoculars and a field guide.

Leaf rubbings

Paper and crayons over leaves create beautiful prints.

Bug safari

Use a magnifying glass to look at insects and spiders. Look, don't touch.

Trail detectives

Look for tracks, scat, chewed nuts and nests. Make up stories about who passed by.

Evening and fireside fun

Storytelling chain

One person starts a story, each person adds a sentence.

Shadow puppets

Use a lantern or headlamp and the side of the tent.

Stargazing

Find the Big Dipper and the North Star. Download a star map before you go. Many parks in West Texas are famous for dark skies.

Camp songs

Songs with call-and-response are perfect for kids.

Cooking as an activity

Cooking at camp is a hands-on activity kids love. Older kids can help with prep, flipping pancakes on a griddle, or assembling foil-free skewers. Toddlers can help season, stir cold ingredients, or set the table.

A compact grill sits low enough for kids to see the cooking but with clear boundaries. Teach them that the grill and everything around it is hot, always.

Rainy-day ideas

  • Cards and board games under the tarp
  • Reading aloud
  • Drawing and journaling
  • A rain walk in rain jackets

Junior ranger programs

Many state and national parks offer junior ranger activity books. Kids complete activities and earn a badge. Ask at the visitor center.

Leave No Trace games

Make "leave what you find" a challenge. Turn trash pick-up into a race. Kids who learn these values early carry them for life.

Questions campers ask

What can kids do while camping?

Scavenger hunts, nature exploration, stargazing, storytelling and cooking are all favorites.

Are there junior ranger programs in parks?

Many state and national parks offer junior ranger activities. Ask at the visitor center.

What should we do when it rains at camp?

Play games under a tarp, read, draw or take a walk in rain gear.
