Questions to ask yourself

  1. How many people do you usually cook for? One to three: Compact. Four or more: Full Size, or two grills.
  2. How do you get to camp? On foot, kayak or small car: Compact. Truck, RV, UTV or boat: either, with the Full Size the natural choice for groups.
  3. Do you cook with cast iron? Both handle it. The Full Size leaves more room beside the pan.
  4. Do you want a fire pit after dinner? Both burn charcoal or cooking wood. The Full Size makes a larger fire for a bigger circle.
  5. How much do you want to carry? About 14 lb versus about 27.6 lb.

Why not just one big grill?

Bulky backyard grills are heavy, need lots of fuel, and eat cargo space. The Slot Grill Full Size gives family-size cooking space in a design that still packs to about an inch and a quarter.

Why not a cheap little camp grill?

Thin, stamped grills wobble, warp, and struggle with cast iron. Both Slot Grill sizes are built from thick carbon steel with no thin stamped sheet metal.

Owning both

Many campers find a place for both: the Compact for quick solo trips and paddling, and the Full Size for family weekends. Set side by side, they give a big cooking area for groups.

Thinking about how you camp

The right size depends less on the grill and more on your trips. Ask yourself:

  • How many people do I usually feed? One to three points to Compact; four or more leans Full Size.
  • How do I travel? Small cars, motorcycles, canoes and short carries favor the lighter Compact.
  • What do I cook? Lots of burgers and steaks for a crowd fit the larger grate better. Breakfast for two fits the smaller one.
  • Do I want it as a fire pit too? Both work, but the bigger firebox makes a larger evening fire.

Weight in real life

The Compact weighs about 14 pounds and the Full Size about 27.6 pounds. Both are heavier than flimsy throwaway grills because they are solid carbon steel, and that weight is what keeps them flat and stable year after year. Fourteen pounds is easy to carry from the car to a walk-in site. Twenty-seven pounds is fine from car to picnic table but noticeable on a long portage.

Cooking area compared

  • Compact: about 146 square inches. Four burgers, two steaks with sides, or breakfast for two or three.
  • Full Size: about 231 square inches. Eight burgers, a family-size spread, or meat and sides at once.

Packing space

Each grill packs into a flat stack, about an inch thick for the Compact and about 1.25 inches for the Full Size. Either slides behind a seat, under a cooler or into a gear bin.

Setup is the same

Both use the same tab-and-slot assembly with no tools, hinges or loose hardware, and both go together in about 30 to 60 seconds. Practice once at home and it becomes second nature.

When to own both

Some campers keep a Compact for solo and couple trips and a Full Size for family weekends. Others run both together at big gatherings, using one for meat and one for sides, or one as a grill and the other as a small fire pit.

Accessories that fit either

The griddle add-on comes in configurations that fit each size, covering half or all of the cooking area. The Seasoning Balm and instant-read thermometer work with either grill.

Quick decision guide

  • Solo, couples, light packers: Compact.
  • Families and groups of four or more: Full Size.
  • Mixed trips: start with the size that matches most outings, or own both.

Still unsure?

Think about the last few trips you took, and the next few you're planning. If most of them were small groups and tight packing, choose the Compact. If they were family weekends and big cooks, choose the Full Size. Either way, you get the same tool-free design, flat packing and clean steel firebox that doubles as a fire pit.

Fire pit size compared

As a fire pit, the Compact makes a small, controlled fire that's ideal for two to four people sitting close. The Full Size holds more fuel and throws warmth to a wider circle for families and groups. Both keep the fire raised off the ground on clean steel instead of a public ring, and both break down flat once the ash is cold. Pick the size that matches the circle of chairs you usually set up around the evening fire.
