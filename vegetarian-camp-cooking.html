Vegetarian Camp Cooking on the Grill

Meat-free camp meals: grilled veggies, halloumi, portobello burgers, and quesadillas.

Vegetarian camp meals can be just as satisfying as meat-based ones. The grill brings out the sweetness in vegetables and adds smoky flavor.

Grilled vegetables

Zucchini, bell peppers, onions, corn, mushrooms and eggplant all grill well. Cut thick, brush with oil, season, and grill over medium heat.

Halloumi skewers

Halloumi holds its shape on the grill. Skewer cubes with vegetables and grill until golden.

Portobello burgers

Marinate portobello caps in olive oil, garlic and balsamic. Grill until tender and serve on buns with cheese.

Quesadillas

Fill tortillas with cheese, beans and grilled vegetables. Toast on a griddle or grate.

Clean grill matters

Vegetarians and vegans especially appreciate a clean grill, since a public grate is coated in strangers' meat drippings. Your own grill solves that.

Planning a meatless camp menu

Vegetarian camp cooking is easier than many people expect, because most produce travels well and does not carry the same spoilage risk as raw meat. Hearty vegetables like peppers, onions, zucchini, corn and potatoes last several days in a cooler. Cheese, eggs and tofu add protein.

Sample three-day vegetarian menu

  • Day 1 dinner: portobello burgers with grilled onions and provolone, grilled corn
  • Day 2 breakfast: griddle pancakes with berries; scrambled eggs with spinach
  • Day 2 lunch: hummus wraps with grilled pepper strips
  • Day 2 dinner: halloumi and vegetable skewers over couscous
  • Day 3 breakfast: breakfast tacos with potatoes, peppers, eggs and cheese
  • Day 3 lunch: bean and cheese quesadillas on the griddle

How to grill each vegetable

  • Zucchini and yellow squash: cut lengthwise into thick planks. Medium heat, 3 to 4 minutes per side.
  • Bell peppers: quarter and seed. Medium-high heat until charred in spots, about 8 minutes.
  • Onions: thick rounds held together with a skewer through the side. Medium heat, 10 minutes, turning once.
  • Corn: in the husk on the cool side for 15 to 20 minutes, or shucked over direct heat for charred kernels.
  • Mushrooms: whole on skewers or in a skillet. Medium heat until browned.
  • Eggplant: thick slices, salted, oiled. Medium heat until tender throughout.
  • Asparagus: lay perpendicular to grate bars, or use a griddle. High heat, 4 to 6 minutes.
  • Sweet potatoes: par-cook at home, then slice and grill until caramelized.

Protein ideas that hold up at camp

  • Halloumi or other grilling cheeses
  • Extra-firm tofu, pressed at home and marinated
  • Canned beans heated in a skillet
  • Eggs for breakfasts and fried rice
  • Tempeh slices brushed with barbecue sauce
  • Store-bought veggie sausages or burgers, kept frozen until needed

A clean surface matters even more

When a campground grate has hosted years of other people's hot dogs and pork chops, there is no telling what is baked onto it. For vegetarian and vegan campers, that is more than a hygiene issue; it is the whole point. Bringing your own grill means your vegetables touch only clean, seasoned steel. A griddle plate adds even more control for delicate items like tofu and sliced mushrooms.

Marinades and sauces to make at home

  • Balsamic herb: balsamic vinegar, olive oil, garlic, dried basil, salt.
  • Chimichurri: parsley, garlic, oregano, red wine vinegar, olive oil, chili flakes.
  • Peanut lime: peanut butter, soy sauce, lime, honey or maple, a splash of water.
  • Yogurt garlic: plain yogurt, garlic, lemon, salt, dill (keep cold).

Packing produce for a cooler

Wash and dry produce at home. Wrap leafy greens in a paper towel inside a bag. Keep tomatoes and avocados on top where they will not get crushed. Store mushrooms in a paper bag inside a container so they do not get slimy.

Troubleshooting vegetarian grilling

  • Vegetables falling through the grate: cut larger or use skewers, a grill basket or a griddle.
  • Tofu sticking: press it well, oil it, and do not flip until it releases.
  • Bland results: salt more than you think and finish with acid like lemon or vinegar.

One-skillet vegetarian meals

  • Veggie fajitas: peppers, onions and mushrooms in a hot skillet with fajita seasoning.
  • Black bean hash: potatoes, black beans, peppers and onions, topped with eggs.
  • Camp fried rice: leftover rice, vegetables, eggs and soy sauce.
  • Skillet pasta: pasta cooked in a pot, tossed with grilled vegetables, olive oil and parmesan.

Vegan camp cooking

  • Use oil instead of butter.
  • Bring plant-based proteins like tofu, tempeh and beans.
  • Pack vegan cheese and yogurt in the cooler.
  • Make sure marinades and breads are vegan.

Grilled pizza on the griddle

Use naan or flatbread. Top with sauce, cheese and grilled vegetables. Heat on the griddle and cover with foil to melt.

Grilled salads

  • Grilled romaine with Caesar dressing
  • Grilled peach and goat cheese salad
  • Charred corn salad with tomatoes and lime

Protein for active trips

Hiking and paddling burn lots of energy. Combine beans and rice, add cheese and eggs, and snack on nuts and seeds.

Cooking for mixed groups

Cook vegetarian items first on a clean grill or keep a dedicated griddle section for them. This avoids cross-contact with meat.

Troubleshooting

  • Veggies soggy: grill hotter and avoid crowding.
  • Mushrooms dry: oil them well.
  • Halloumi too salty: soak in water briefly before grilling.

A three-day vegetarian camp menu

Day one - Lunch: hummus and veggie wraps made at home. - Dinner: grilled halloumi skewers with peppers and red onion, couscous made with hot water from the grill, and a cucumber salad.

Day two - Breakfast: griddle potatoes with peppers, onions and fried eggs. - Lunch: bean and cheese quesadillas crisped on the griddle. - Dinner: grilled portobello caps with garlic oil, corn on the cob and foil-pack sweet potatoes.

Day three - Breakfast: banana pancakes with peanut butter. - Lunch: leftover grilled vegetables tucked into pita with feta.

Tofu that actually works over coals

Tofu has a reputation for falling apart on a grill, but extra-firm tofu holds up well with a little prep.

  1. Press the block at home between towels under a heavy pan for half an hour.
  2. Slice into thick slabs, about three-quarters of an inch.
  3. Marinate in a bag with soy sauce, garlic, a little sugar and oil.
  4. Grill over medium heat on a well-oiled grate, or on the griddle, without moving it until it releases.
  5. Flip once, brush with more marinade, and serve when both sides are browned.

Building flavor without meat

  • Smoke: smoked paprika, chipotle and a few minutes on a hot grate add depth.
  • Umami: soy sauce, miso, mushrooms, parmesan and tomato paste.
  • Fat: good olive oil, butter, avocado and nuts make vegetable meals satisfying.
  • Acid: a squeeze of lime or a splash of vinegar at the end brightens everything.
  • Crunch: toasted seeds, crushed tortilla chips or fried shallots from a jar.

Keeping vegetables fresh in the cooler

Hardy vegetables like carrots, cabbage, peppers, onions, potatoes and squash last the whole trip. Delicate greens and berries should be eaten in the first day or two. Keep produce in a separate bag above the ice so it doesn't get waterlogged.

Why a dedicated grill helps vegetarians

On a shared public grate, vegetables land on residue from whatever meat was cooked there before. Bringing your own clean, seasoned grill means the surface is yours, and if you share with meat-eaters, you can cook the vegetarian food first.

Easy vegetarian sides for any meal

  • Grilled corn with chili-lime butter
  • Charred broccoli with lemon and parmesan
  • Grilled sweet potato rounds with honey
  • Warm flatbread brushed with garlic oil
  • Foil-pack green beans with almonds
  • Black bean salad with corn and peppers made at home

These sides round out a meat-free meal or give vegetarians plenty of options at a mixed-group cookout.

Breakfast without bacon

Vegetarian camp breakfasts can be just as hearty. Try griddle potatoes with peppers and eggs, banana pancakes with peanut butter, breakfast tacos with black beans and salsa, or a skillet of sautéed mushrooms and spinach topped with feta and fried eggs. Warm tortillas on the grate and set out hot sauce, and nobody will miss the meat on a cool morning.

Questions campers ask

What vegetables grill best at camp?

Zucchini, peppers, onions, corn, mushrooms and eggplant.

Can I grill cheese?

Halloumi and similar grilling cheeses hold their shape.

Why bring my own grill as a vegetarian?

Public grates often carry meat residue from previous users.

How long do vegetables last in a camping cooler?

Hearty vegetables like peppers, onions, corn and potatoes typically last several days if kept cold and dry.

How do I grill tofu at camp?

Press extra-firm tofu at home, marinate, oil well and grill or griddle without flipping until it releases.
